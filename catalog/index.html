Head, face or neck

Early Neck Strengthening

pdf-neck-01 · Source describes a baby

Read source description

Lie your baby on your lap with their head away from you. Gently lift their shoulders up to bring them into a sitting position. Do this gently and slowly to allow them to activate their neck muscles. If your baby lacks the necessary neck strength by letting their head tilt back too far, bend your knees more so they are closer to a sitting position.

Source describes a baby. Pediatric clinician assessment and caregiver instruction are required; exact age limits are not supplied.

Neck Rotation Against Adult

pdf-neck-03 · Source describes a baby

Read source description

Hold your baby facing you and against you with their head turned to the restricted side. Support your baby's head in that position for a short moment before moving your baby away and letting them freely move their head and neck. This exercise will help to improve rotation in the neck, especially useful in cases of torticollis.

Neck Rotation Supine Stretch

pdf-neck-04 · Source describes a baby

Read source description

Lie your baby on their back on a soft flat surface. Let their arms find their natural position. Turn their head to the restricted side and hold with one hand. At the same time stabilise the shoulder with the other hand, to make sure it does not lift off the floor as you turn the head. Your baby will not tolerate this for long, so little and often usually works well. This exercise will help to improve rotation in the neck, especially useful in cases of torticollis.

Source describes a baby. Pediatric clinician assessment and caregiver instruction are required; exact age limits are not supplied.

Neck Rotations Standing

pdf-neck-05 · Age suitability not established

Read source description

Stand upright with good posture, holding on to a wall or table if required. Rotate your neck slowly to the left by looking over your left shoulder. Take your neck to a comfortable end of range. Repeat to the right. Make sure you keep your shoulder and back relaxed. This is an excellent exercise to improve rotation and mobility in your neck, as well as improving your balance.

Reach Outs and Self Hugs

pdf-core-09 · Age suitability not established

Read source description

Stand in a neutral position, unlock both your knees a little. Slowly breathe in and spread your arms straight out sideways into a wide arm stretch. Drop your weight back into your heels. Lightly squeeze into the S-shape of your spine at the back. Relax and pause here for a few seconds with a good overall balanced stretch from your hands and across the front of your chest. Breathe in a little more deeply into the stretch to further open up the ribs and abdomen. Be gentle and don’t push too hard. Breathe out in this position. Take another gentle deep breath here, hold your breath for a few seconds, open everything up a little more, relax in the position. (In this position, you also have the option here to further explore stretching out your front or squeezing out your back). Let go of your breath slowly through your nose and bring your hands and elbows across each other smoothly into a V-shape across your chest, holding onto each opposite shoulder in a self-hugging position. Drop your knees a little, drop your head forwards and let your spine curl into a C-shape. Slowly breathe into the self-hug and allow your mid-back to stretch backwards. Relax and pause yourself for a few seconds in this position. (In this position, you also have the option here to further explore stretching out your back or squeezing out your front). Let go of the breath slowly through your nose, come back towards the starting position. Repeat the whole process in a smooth continuous cycle.

No exercise-specific age range is supplied. Suitability requires clinician review; age alone is not clearance.
